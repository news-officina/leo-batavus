Meta, Google and TikTok sue to block California ban on teen feeds

The three companies filed separate lawsuits on November 4 against Assembly Bill 1709, which takes effect on January 1, 2027. Attorney General Rob Bonta says his office will defend the law.

6 November 2026

Meta, Google and TikTok are suing to strike down California's ban on addictive feeds for under-16s before it takes effect on January 1, 2027. The three companies filed separate lawsuits on November 4, asking courts to block Attorney General Rob Bonta from enforcing Assembly Bill 1709.

Meta says the law would force it to strip away age-appropriate features or cut thousands of California teens off from family and friends. A Meta spokesperson said the First Amendment bars the government from dictating how ideas are organized, displayed or shared with Californians. TikTok calls it a blatantly unconstitutional restriction on free expression that takes away parents' rights to decide what suits their kids. Google says parents are best positioned to make these choices, through the protections and tools it already offers.

Meta argues that its feeds get First Amendment protection under Moody v. NetChoice, the 2024 Supreme Court ruling that social media platforms may decide how they display and order content. Meta also claims Section 230, the US law shielding platforms from liability over user content, preempts the bill.

Governor Gavin Newsom signed the bill into law on September 10. In August 2026, US District Judge Edward Davila refused to block Senate Bill 976, a comparable law. He also rejected their bid to keep it from taking effect while they sought Ninth Circuit review.

Bonta's office says it is committed to defending California's "commonsense laws to protect kids online" and is reviewing the lawsuits, a representative said. Critics, among the Electronic Privacy Information Center, say the suits rehash arguments a district court rejected in August.
