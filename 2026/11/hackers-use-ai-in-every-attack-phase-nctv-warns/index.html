Hackers use AI in every attack phase, NCTV warns

The NCTV says a disruptive attack on Dutch society is a matter of when, not whether, in its yearly threat report published on Friday. The cabinet answered with a new action programme the same day.

7 November 2026, updated 8 November 2026

Hackers now use artificial intelligence in every phase of their attacks, the NCTV says. It reports this for the first time in its Cybersecuritybeeld Nederland 2026, its yearly overview of digital threats, published on Friday 6 November. Its warning is blunt: it is not a question of whether but when the country faces an attack that disrupts society.

The NCTV says basic measures are still not in order, despite years of warnings. Hackers have already hit Dutch targets. ShinyHunters stole data from more than 6 million Odido customers. Attackers reached employee data at the prison service, the data protection authority and the council for the judiciary. Under the new Cyberbeveiligingswet law, administrators of covered organisations now carry the responsibility, the AIVD notes.

The report names Russia. According to the NCTV, Russian hackers tried to gain access to the Signal and WhatsApp accounts of Dutch civil servants. Russian state hackers also targeted Dutch IP cameras that are accessible online, the agency reports. Their aim was to collect militarily relevant data, such as military transport routes and the logistics of matériel deliveries to Ukraine.

The gap is widening, the NCTV concludes: attackers grow stronger while the digital resilience of Dutch organisations does not keep pace. China, Russia, Iran and North Korea have set their sights on the Netherlands. Russian groups attack Dutch and European targets continuously, gathering intelligence on the war in Ukraine and sowing unrest among NATO allies. The time between discovery and abuse of a vulnerability has fallen to a few hours. The NCTV wants patching times cut drastically.

The cabinet answered the same day. Coordinated by the NCTV, it presented the actieprogramma toekomstbestendige digitale weerbaarheid, an action programme to make digital resilience future-proof. The programme includes an AI-wasstraat, a digital wash cycle that screens systems for vulnerabilities, and better preparation of small and medium-sized businesses for incidents. NCTV coordinator Marc Kuipers called the news very bad, worse than ever before. The Netherlands is like frogs in a pan of water, he said, whose temperature rises a degree every day. It should not wait for a conflict with Russia before acting.
