Ten JEF countries to sign naval partnership pact on 10 November

The agreement commits the navies, including the Dutch, to joint doctrine, training and planning to deter Russia. It is meant to complement NATO as the United States reduces the forces it would send to Europe in a crisis.

9 November 2026

The ten countries of the Joint Expeditionary Force will sign an agreement on naval partnership on 10 November, to deter Russia from military aggression in the region. Under the agreement, the navies commit to aligning doctrine, training, planning, sustainment and capability development. The United Kingdom's Royal Navy set out the principle in July as the Maritime Proposition.

British First Sea Lord General Gwyn Jenkins said the joint force would complement NATO and respond to crises faster. He said the joint forces would be commanded from the Northwood main maritime headquarters in London. In July, Washington informed European allies it would cut the fighter aircraft it would make available to NATO in a crisis by a third, and the strategic bombers by half. It would no longer provide submarines.

The Joint Expeditionary Force brings together Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Iceland, Latvia, Lithuania, the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden and the United Kingdom. It was Norway that proposed the initiative to establish a broad maritime partnership among the members. On 9 July, all ten nations formally agreed to the framework.

The partnership is meant to strengthen deterrence along the sea border with Russia and support NATO. Jenkins said in July that the joint force would also give political leaders real options in times of crisis and conflict. The United States will not take part in the joint naval forces, according to Russian reports.

Canada has applied to join the JEF Framework, and the force's defence ministers have advanced work towards a shared recommendation on Canadian membership. The ministers also stated in October that JEF nations do not seek confrontation and pose no threat to Russia, but that Russia should be in no doubt that they will defend themselves.
